# Changelog

What changed in each update, in the player's words. The game shows the same notes in its
What's new window (Menu, or once after an update), from `src/data/patchnotes.ts`.

## 0.3: Letters back, a week of visitors, and an ending (2026-11-08)

### New
- An ending: when the Grand Oceanarium opens, a short finale plays and Ida writes one last
  letter, before any other Oceanarium letter, even if you get there overnight. The shop carries
  on afterwards.
- Letters write back. Every correspondent has a little story now: replies quote the tank that
  answered them, promises are kept, and each finished story leaves a keepsake. The Codex keeps
  them all under Plaques, and where the screen has room they stand on a shelf in the hall.
- A week. Tuesday is Dr. Reyes's bench, Friday is Room 4's school trip, Saturday is market day
  and Sunday is the Driftwood Club's judging. Hover the clock to see what is on.
- Conservation projects: spend spare coins on river clean-ups, a coral nursery, a marine reserve
  and more, for reputation and a word from the people running them.
- Rays: the blue-spotted ribbontail ray and the cownose ray, which glides over the sand in a
  school, and a Ray Lagoon to keep them in.
- More letters for the late game, codex milestones and plaques, and a Cleaning Crew upgrade that
  wipes the glass for you.
- The plant dip catches hitchhiking snails at the door, and Pip still writes about them. The
  Driftwood Club only judges a real scape.
- Click the Closed sign to sleep until opening time (once Ida's tour is over).
- A What's new window, under Menu any time.

### Fishkeeping
- Reef tanks settle at a reef pH on live sand, so saltwater animals are no longer "too alkaline".
- Chillers are rated in gallons like heaters, with one at every tier that sells cold-water
  animals. An exhibit heat exchanger and chiller plant keep the big exhibits right.
- Jelly kreisels work: jellies make little waste, and open water no longer costs scape points
  where the tank type wants it.
- Kelp grows in the kelp tower, its floats holding it up to the light, and urchins leave it alone,
  the deep sea chamber's cast can live together, and the touch pool's animals no longer eat each
  other.
- Driftwood leaches less as it soaks but keeps a lasting tint. A handful of catappa leaves tints
  a small tank amber and a bed of them keeps it blackwater; activated carbon clears a handful. The
  first time the water stains, the shop tells you why.
- Breeding stays within what the filter and the water can hold. Stock racks keep a breeding group
  of their best animals back, so colonies keep breeding, and the rack card says how many of each it
  keeps and why any are not for sale. Feeding once a day is fine.
- Species arrive at the tier where a tank big enough for them is sold. Peat keeps soft-water fish
  happy from the start, and brackish fish have real salinity ranges.
- Taking out a stone or wood puts the plants growing on it into storage instead of the bin.

### Help and feedback
- "Good fit" now looks at where the water is heading (stones, soil, wood, the heater) and says
  why a fish would not fit.
- Hall cards show small warnings for unwell fish, bad water, hunger and green glass, and a note
  pops up when a tank takes a turn for the worse.
- A little celebration when a tank gains a star. The Scape tab shows the score change as you
  place things.
- The order list and the codex show where each species lives (for the ones you have not met yet,
  once Dr. Reyes shares her notebooks). The order list has filters and a biotope helper, and
  letters show your progress with a How? button into the codex.
- Greyed-out buttons say why. The report card names the worst problem first. Bioload, PAR and
  flow have plain-words tooltips.
- Ida's tour picks the right tank size, no longer scolds the first feeding, and its pointer stays
  clear of other buttons.

### Controls and screens
- Touch works: tap, hold for help, drag to scroll lists, two fingers to pan and pinch.
- Sharper pixels with 125% and 150% Windows scaling, a bigger tank view on laptops, and a
  friendly "turn your phone sideways" card.
- Shortcuts leave Ctrl and Cmd combinations to the browser and ignore Caps Lock. Space resumes
  your last speed.
- Watch mode is just for watching, with a Back button. Drags can no longer teleport pieces, and
  plants stay on the wood you move.
- Long letters scroll clear of their stamp, the codex draws big animals big, names like Tomás keep
  their accents, and the new-chapter banner steps aside for the notice under it.
- A fuller sound mix, and the murmur of visitors no longer loops.
- Saves are safer: a save that cannot be read is kept and backed up, never wiped.
- Shops from before this update keep their Conservation Fund work as funded projects, get the
  codex plaques they have already earned, and read replies that name the right fish.
